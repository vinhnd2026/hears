Food processing weighing

Mixers, blending tanks and storage vessels in a food plant are washed down constantly — so the weighing underneath them lives in water, steam and detergent. Food processing is one of Vietnam’s largest industries, and its weighing points are among the wettest anywhere.

Request a quoteThe pain points

The weighing challenges in food plants

Hygiene rules set the environment; the weighing has to survive the same regime as the vessels it carries.

  • Washdown, every shift

    Water, steam and cleaning chemicals reach everything near a vessel — load cells, junction boxes and cables included.

  • Hygiene and cleanability

    Smooth surfaces and few dirt traps make cleaning faster and audits easier; crevices and cable entries around sensors work against both.

  • Hard-to-reach vessels

    Tanks in tight halls are difficult and disruptive to service — the less equipment there is at the vessel, the better.

  • Condensation and cold rooms

    Temperature swings condense moisture inside enclosures and junction boxes — the slow failure nobody sees until the reading drifts.

The hydrostatic answer

A hydrostatic load cell is a sealed stainless pressure capsule: no strain gauges, no cables, no junction box at the vessel. The signal travels as fluid pressure through tubing to a totalizer in a dry area. Emery Winslow’s food-plant record is published — mixers, hoppers and massagers at Plumrose, Morgan Foods and Boar’s Head run on hydrostatic cells precisely because of nightly high-pressure washdown. Those cells carry Emery Winslow's lifetime guarantee against water, flooding, humidity, washdown and lightning damage.

  • No electronics at the vessel to wash, condense on or corrode
  • Stainless cells that tolerate detergents and steam
  • All electronics in a clean, dry room
  • HearS design, installation and calibration support in Vietnam

Where it fits

  • Mixing and blending vessels

    Recipe batching by weight instead of level estimates — the vessel itself becomes the scale.

  • Storage and buffer tanks

    Ingredient and product inventory by weight, without manual dipping, sight glasses or guesswork.

  • Sugar, brewing and silos

    Raw sugar and grain silos, malt and adjunct tanks, fermenters and bright tanks weighed through the process.

The digital layer: every weighing becomes a record

Batch weighing becomes batch records: each ingredient addition logged against its recipe and lot. That gives production its traceability file — and gives quality control the numbers to release or hold a batch — without a second, manual record kept beside the scale.
